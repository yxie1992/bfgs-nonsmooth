## What to change

Kinds (field `kind`):
- `caveat`: a sentence or clause whose only job is to say what a result does not claim, prove,
  mean or establish ("This does not prove ...", "This is not a claim that ...", "X is not asserted",
  "must not be read as", "must not silently be strengthened", "no claim combines ...", "This is not
  a ... certification", "not a genericity or necessity claim", "not a new run count"). Delete it.
- `repeat`: a scope point that Chapter 1 (`01_I.tex`) states once, repeated in a later chapter only
  as a hedge. Delete the repeat unless the chapter's own argument uses it. The scope points are:
  (a) positive theorems quantify over infinite admissible runs; their existence is a hypothesis;
  (b) the counterexamples give deterministic failing starts arbitrarily close to the minimizer;
  positive-measure / almost-sure failure is a separate open question;
  (c) certificates, comparison fields and energies are used only in the proofs (BFGS never
  evaluates them);
  (d) the conditions are sufficient, not necessary; nonnecessity examples are in Chapter G;
  (e) rounded numerical points are not claimed to lie on the stable manifold; numerics are not used
  as proofs; (f) no finite oracle complexity or rates beyond those stated.
- `guard`: guard words and emphasis that only signal caution: "actual(ly)", "genuine(ly)",
  "operative", "true" or "exactly"/"precisely" used only for emphasis, bold face on a negative
  statement (`\textbf{not ...}`), "explicitly disclosed", "unsupported historical". Remove the word or
  the bold face. Keep "exact" when it is mathematical (exact identity, exact rational certificate,
  exact closure), and keep "true minimizer" where it distinguishes the minimizer from a spurious
  limit point.
- `positive`: a sentence of the form "X; it is not Y", "X, not Y", "X rather than Y", "Y does not
  subsume X", "X is not superseded by Y", "X remains independent/separate", or a defensive prior-work
  attribution ("is prior work, not a new result of this report", "not new discoveries here").
  Rewrite as a positive statement of X (for attributions: "... is due to Guo--Lewis~\cite{GL18}").
  The companion is a consolidation of earlier research notes and records which result supersedes
  which; keep such supersession facts, but drop defensive framing ("must both be retained",
  "retained independently", "is not a separate strongest theorem").

## What to keep (do not propose changes)

- Every theorem-like environment (theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary, definition, remark, ...),
  every display (`\[...\]`, equation, align, gather, ...), every manual result heading
  (`\textbf{Theorem ...}`), the hypotheses and conclusions of every result, and the mathematical
  argument of every proof. Never weaken or strengthen a claim and never add a new mathematical claim:
  a positive rewrite restates content that is already there.
- Explicit open questions and conjectures ("... remains open", "Whether ... is open"). You may make
  the phrasing plain (no bold, no "has not been proved" framing, e.g. "Whether ... is open."), but
  keep the question.
- Generality remarks, which promote a result: "No continuity, uniform boundedness, or positive
  definiteness of W is required.", "Branches with b_i=0 are allowed.", "The algorithm need not
  identify a cell.", "No damping, restart, ... is introduced."
- Explanations that answer a question a reader would naturally ask: why a counterexample does not
  contradict a positive theorem, which hypothesis it violates, how two results differ in their
  hypotheses, why a construction needs a particular step, that global failure "is consistent with
  local convergence near the true minimizer". Shorten them if they are verbose; keep them.
- Sharpness and necessity results that the companion proves (e.g. "The inclusion of RS is strict",
  nonnecessity examples of Chapter G, "flat contours put this family outside the strict-contour
  theorem" backed by an example). These are results; at most rephrase them positively.
- In the verification chapter (14_E) and other process passages: factual statements of what was
  checked, how, and where (commands, counts, files, dates). Delete only hedges.
- Handled by the coordinator (the validator rejects edits there): lines 1-3 of each chapter (the
  chapter introduction), the sentence "The following comparisons are bibliographic scholarship,
  separate from the formal theorem assertions.", the sentences beginning "Lewis--Overton~\cite[Section~5.1]{LO}
  analyzes the scalar absolute-value model" and "The Euclidean-norm theorem is a known case recovered
  here.", and in `07_H.tex` the passage from "Existence of an infinite admissible run is a separate
  issue." to "remains open here.". Never touch a line starting with `% SOURCE_BEGIN` or `% SOURCE_END`.
- In `13_L.tex`, edits in the paragraph containing "The first three branches of the local
  finite-branch construction fail to be twice differentiable" are allowed but must be flagged in
  the reason ("ROOT PARAGRAPH"); the coordinator then adds a correspondence record.

Use judgment: the goal is a confident, readable text. Do not delete content a reader needs, and do
not invent new content. When in doubt, keep and list the case under "borderline" in your report.

## Output

Write `/tmp/opencode/m44/companion/proposals_<GROUP>.json`:

```json
{"group": "A",
 "proposals": [
  {"id": "A-02M-01",
   "file": "companion/chapters/02_M.tex",
   "kind": "caveat",
   "old": "exact substring of the chapter file, occurring exactly once",
   "new": "replacement text ('' to delete)",
   "reason": "one sentence",
   "register": [
     {"item": "C/02_M/prose/017", "status": "NON_CLAIM", "action": "retire", "note": "..."},
     {"item": "C/02_M/prose/018", "status": "LEAN", "action": "reanchor",
      "anchor": "new verbatim anchor from the new text", "note": "..."}
   ]}
 ]}
```

- `old` is matched exactly (not whitespace-normalized). Paragraphs are mostly single long lines, so
  `old` is normally a sentence inside a line. When deleting a sentence, include exactly one of the
  adjacent spaces in `old` so that no double space remains; when deleting a whole paragraph, include
  one of the surrounding blank lines.
- Proposals of one file must not overlap. Use separate proposals for separate sentences.
- `register`: the validator lists, for each proposal, the register prose items whose anchor `old`
  overlaps and the item that probably contains the edited sentence. For each affected item give
  `action`:
  - `retire`: the item's claim is deleted entirely. Allowed for NON_CLAIM and SAME_AS items. Avoid it
    for LEAN, OPEN, DEFINITION, PART_OF and PROOF items; if unavoidable, explain in `note` which
    surviving item states the same claim. Look for references to the item:
    `grep -rn "<item id>" editorial/lean_coverage/register bfgs_lean_article/BfgsArticle` and list
    them in `note`.
  - `reanchor`: the item's sentence is rewritten or its anchor text was edited, but the claim
    remains. Give `anchor`: a short verbatim quote (about 6-15 words) of the new text that lies in
    the item's sentence. Mathematical content of a LEAN item must not change.
  - `keep`: the anchor survives unchanged and the claim is unchanged.
  Ignore line numbers; the coordinator recomputes them.

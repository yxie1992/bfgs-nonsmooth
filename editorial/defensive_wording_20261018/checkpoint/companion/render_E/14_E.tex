\section{What the checks mean}\label{what-the-checks-mean}

Lean checks the formal proof term against its precise statement. Exact rational inequalities, algebraic roots, matrix updates, objective derivatives, infinite sequences and geometric existence arguments have different mathematical roles, but each retained assertion here has a reviewed formal correspondence. Compiler logs are evidence of the executions they record.

The principal results concern ordinary full BFGS. The negative seeds and their embeddings are explicit objective/run constructions. The positive framework derives its scalar hierarchy from the principal determinants of the full-BFGS matrices. The stable-manifold conclusion in Chapter~\ref{comp:L} follows from the return map and a proved analytic argument. Exact formal hypotheses and dependencies appear in the complete claim index.

\section{Reproduce the audit}\label{reproduce-the-audit}

From \path{bfgs_lean_stage1/}, the original pinned bootstrap remains the entry point for obtaining the toolchain and building the project:
\begin{verbatim}
bash scripts/bootstrap.sh
\end{verbatim}
The closed milestone evidence should be preserved when rerunning tools that write their historical output paths. The current edition's public runner instead creates fresh output directories:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 master_report/audit/run.py --run
\end{verbatim}
The audit records the commands and compiler output, checks the main source hashes, verifies the pinned clean dependencies before and after, enumerates the compiled module-owned declarations, and reconciles the main and supplemental environments. Its README describes the read-only evidence check; the runner prints the new timestamped output directory. A nonzero exit or mismatch fails the gate.

The earlier source/correspondence records also have read-only validators:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 scripts/validate_report_reconstruction_19.py
python3 scripts/verify_report_claim_review_20.py
\end{verbatim}
The axiom audit verifies proof dependencies. No external symbolic, interval or numerical result is a trusted mathematical input.

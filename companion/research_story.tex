The question is what convergence ordinary full BFGS can guarantee when a
convex objective has gradient jumps. Its practical success invites a
theory, but the smooth argument cannot simply treat every secant as a
bounded Hessian acting on a short step. Lewis--Overton's experiments and
Challenge~7.1 place this problem in a broader almost-sure semialgebraic
setting~\cite{LO}. The present work gives deterministic positive and negative
answers for specified convex classes. It does not claim to settle every
part of that challenge.

The mixed objective $F(u,v)=u^2+|v|$ gives a concrete measure of progress.
Guo--Lewis developed extensions of Powell's smooth-convex argument and
proved convergence on the Euclidean norm~\cite[Theorems~3.2, 4.1, 4.3 and
Corollary~4.2]{GL18}. Their Corollary~4.4 proves only that a subsequence for
the mixed objective approaches a point on $v=0$, leaving whole-sequence
convergence to zero open. The horizontal error $u^2$ can remain positive on
that line. Gebken's published 2026 introduction still identifies this
benchmark as unresolved, while its criticality theorem assumes iterate
and matrix behavior~\cite[Theorem~3.1 and (B1)]{BG}.

The centered smooth--sublinear result here proves that every infinite
admissible full-BFGS run on $u^2+|v|$ converges to zero, for arbitrary
positive-definite initialization and fixed valid Armijo--weak-Wolfe
parameters. It is a consequence of a family argument in arbitrary dimension.
The sublinear part is modeled exactly toward its center; adding a locally
$C^{1,1}$ convex term leaves only a quadratic inward remainder. Compact
initial sublevels inside the structural region give value convergence,
and uniqueness gives convergence of the whole sequence. The same route
covers coercive degree-one homogeneous functions, including coercive finite
maxima of linear functions. The Euclidean norm is an attributed known case.
The full noncompact homogeneous value alternatives are retained separately:
value convergence does not imply bounded iterates, and it does not subsume
Xie--W\"achter's finite detection of unbounded descent in the two-piece
affine model~\cite[Theorem~IV.1]{XW}.

Local convergence extends this positive picture. Finitely many polyhedral
cells with smooth extensions supply a uniform inward model after a low
sublevel traps the iterates near the minimizer. The algorithm need not
identify a cell. Curved switching sets can defeat that radial condition;
the planar theorem constructs a comparison field along positive contours
instead. Its nondegenerate smooth-max corollary and explicit three-quadratic
example show a convergence conclusion beyond the radial class. The
fixed-normal theorem in arbitrary dimension and the flat-contour parabolic
family use different hypotheses and remain independent parts of the theory.

The negative results establish a complementary limitation. A fixed unit-first
implementation can generate a shrinking cycle around a nonstationary corner.
The construction certifies every rejected and accepted query, and then embeds
copies so that one fixed objective has failing starts arbitrarily close to
its unique minimizer, all with identity initialization. One embedding has
four globally convex $C^{2,1}$ branches with bounded Hessians and no uniform
positive lower-curvature assumption. A different embedding preserves uniform
strong convexity with $C^{1,1}$ branches that lose second differentiability at
the accumulation point. These are two distinct exclusions of deterministic
neighborhood guarantees. They neither establish positive-probability failure
nor produce a finite uniformly strongly convex $C^2$ local counterexample.

The common analytical tool is an adjacent-degree hierarchy for actual
principal determinants of full-BFGS matrices. Building on the classical
trace/determinant approach~\cite{FND_P76,FND_BN89,FND_N92}, it forces the
arithmetic mean of reciprocal metric-gradient values to diverge under the
stated bounded-gradient and vanishing-decrease hypotheses. A comparison
energy turns this matrix restriction into convergence when objective
geometry preserves visibility of nonoptimality, controls the secant
correction and permits transport at a summable cost. Matrix degeneration
is allowed throughout. The radial and moving-field arguments demonstrate
ways this mechanism succeeds; the scalar nonnecessity results prevent it
from being read as a complete characterization.

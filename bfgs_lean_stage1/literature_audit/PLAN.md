# Literature and proof-precedent audit plan

Compare the retained corrected mathematical results with primary literature,
including journal papers, author manuscripts, theses and dated preprints. Treat
journal publication and first public preprint dates separately. The search covers
positive convergence results, negative BFGS examples, certificate limitations,
quantitative matrix claims, and the proof techniques supporting them.

1. Freeze the corrected edition and enumerate research claims and report spans.
2. Search four parallel groups: D/L negative examples; C/Q/U/A geometry;
   R/F/H and scalar positive families; M/T/G matrix and certificate mechanisms.
3. Read the closest primary sources at theorem/proof level. Compare objective
   regularity, local/global conclusion, line search, actual full BFGS, admissible
   run quantifiers, matrix assumptions, and deterministic versus chosen lengths.
4. Record exact overlap, special cases, broader/narrower results, established
   techniques, unavailable full texts, and searches with no exact match found.
   A failed search is not proof of novelty. Similarity is not proof that AI text
   actually used a source. Already cited works and newly found precedents are
   identified separately.
5. Reconcile the packets into a comprehensive Markdown report, machine-readable
   claim/source registers, search logs, technique map and recommended citations.
6. Independently review material classifications and source locators, verify the
   edition remains unchanged, then commit and push the final evidence milestone.

All artifacts live in this directory. The released Lean edition and its existing
manifest remain unchanged. Downloaded papers, if needed for reading, are working
evidence only and are not redistributed in the public deliverable; bibliography,
short source-specific evidence summaries, locators and hashes are retained.

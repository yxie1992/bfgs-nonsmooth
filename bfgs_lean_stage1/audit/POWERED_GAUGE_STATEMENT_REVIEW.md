## Hypothesis and interpretation checks

- The full objective is finite (`Vec n → ℝ`) and convex. The component gauges are finite, convex, nonnegative, and positively degree-one homogeneous. The smooth summand is locally C1,1 on an open representation neighborhood. Its separate convexity is not needed in this proof: the full objective's convexity supplies the lower supporting-error bound. Thus the theorem applies in particular under the source's convex-smooth-summand hypothesis, without strengthening it.
- The source writes the smooth summand in coordinates centered at the optimizer, `F(star+z)=ψ(z)+…`; the formal statement writes that same summand in original coordinates, `F(x)=ψ(x)+…(x-star)`. This is a coordinate convention for the supplied local representation and does not invoke affine or nonlinear invariance of BFGS. A separate smoothness-translation wrapper is not claimed by this unit.
- The theorem does not assert that mixed gauge sums automatically have a minimizer, unique minimizer, or compact sublevel. Those are the explicit source provisos. The local unique-minimizer corollary derives compactness instead of adding it as a premise.
- The actual full spatial Hessian interpretation, absence of singular radial curvature in other families, and a general central-difference equivalence are separate R statements. They are not assumptions of this gauge proof, and this unit does not claim to verify them. Named spectral/nuclear norm instances likewise require their own representation/type bridges; the abstract finite-gauge theorem includes them only once those standard properties are supplied.

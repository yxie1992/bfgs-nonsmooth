Reviewed `FixedNormalCutoff`, `FixedNormalVisibility`, `FixedNormalSmoothStrip`, `FixedNormalConvergence`, and `FixedNormalSourceWrappers` against U.1, U.2, and the common proof in U.5, following the earlier four-module core review. No mathematical or statement defect was found in the strong-convexity principal tranche.

The actual-run theorems assume the source objective structure on an open neighborhood of a compact trapping sublevel: finite convex F, positive Euclidean strong convexity, one fixed Euclidean unit normal, common tangential projections of every actual supporting subgradient, a Lipschitz tangential field, and local C1,1 only off the specified closed exceptional scalar set. `EuclideanStrongConvexOn` is convexity of the actual function minus the Euclidean quadratic and therefore includes convexity of the neighborhood. The norm on Lipschitz constants is the coordinate Pi norm; the estimates include the corresponding finite-dimensional factors and do not identify it with the Euclidean norm. This is the same qualitative Lipschitz hypothesis.

The U.1 theorem does not assume every point on the exceptional hyperplane is nonsmooth. The optimizer lies on that plane, and strong support monotonicity derives that a zero tangential projection there forces that optimizer. By contrast, U.2 explicitly assumes actual nondifferentiability at every exceptional point, which is needed for the proved affine-hull equivalence. Off the exceptional set, the supplied local C1,1 property implies actual differentiability. The offset parameter b in U.2 is a harmless generalization of the source's zero-offset coordinate; taking b=0 gives the printed theorem. The affine-critical cluster theorem does not separately require an attained-minimum premise, so its source instance is covered without adding assumptions.

The cutoff is constructed from the scalar image of the entire compact cluster-set tangential-zero set. Both empty exceptional sets and empty tangential-zero sets are explicitly handled before using distance to a nonempty set. The construction produces a global Lipschitz function in [0,1], equal to one on the required compact set and identically zero on a positive-width neighborhood of every exceptional scalar level. There is no presumed limiting iterate or single-cluster localization.

The masked normal derivative is proved continuous across the interfaces because it is identically zero nearby, while the full derivative is used only on the open regular region. Compactness supplies a strictly positive visibility constant on an open neighborhood of the whole cluster set, and the actual trajectory is proved eventually to enter that neighborhood. No continuity of the full nonsmooth gradient is assumed.

The smooth-strip bound applies when the destination cutoff is nonzero. That condition derives a uniform positive distance from all exceptional levels. A compact cover then gives full-ball derivative Lipschitz estimates, including the source endpoint. Strong convexity converts the squared displacement estimate to the actual curvature denominator. The zero-cutoff case instead uses only the tangential estimate. Thus no global full-gradient Lipschitz constant or bounded behavior of such constants approaching the interfaces is present.

The final contradiction constructs an actual orthonormal tangent matrix, actual cutoff frame, actual divided-difference transport coefficient, and actual tangential residual. It uses the previously reviewed zero-normal-step-safe identity. Strong convexity derives displacement-square summability and the small-step tail; Armijo plus compact trapping derives predicted-decrease summability and gradient boundedness. The residual-frame theorem then derives the required Hessian/reciprocal-budget contradiction for unmodified full BFGS. The hypotheses contain no assumed frame certificate, trace recurrence, Hessian growth rate, iterate convergence, matrix convergence, or step convergence.

`local_hyperplane_convergence_basin` and `local_parallel_interfaces_convergence_basin` place the existential positive objective threshold before the universal actual run. The threshold is selected only from the objective's unique optimizer and structural neighborhood, so it is independent of H0 and the valid fixed Armijo/weak-Wolfe parameters carried by the run. Compactness of the low initial sublevel and its inclusion in the structural neighborhood are derived. The source-level trapping-sublevel wrapper also derives compactness of the smaller run sublevel from the given larger one.

The affine-critical correctness corollary uses actual supporting-subgradient membership of zero to obtain the true global minimum. It proves minimum-value convergence and actual solution-set distance convergence, and requests uniqueness only for whole-iterate convergence. These conclusions do not smuggle the desired optimizer identification into the U.2 cluster theorem.

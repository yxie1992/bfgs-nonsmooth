## Premise checks that matter

`FullBFGSRun` is an algebraic interface that includes positive definiteness, positive energy, and positive curvature at every index. Reading only this structure could incorrectly suggest that preservation has been assumed. The actual objective-level structure, `AdmissibleBFGSRun`, assumes SPD only at index zero, nonzero accepted gradients, positive lengths, the actual weak-Wolfe inequality, and the full update. `fullBFGS_positive_invariants` proves the later SPD, energy, and curvature facts by induction. `ObjectiveRun.toFullBFGSRun` transfers the identical matrices, gradients, and lengths with those derived facts. The review additionally constructs `rawRun` and proves all five raw growth contracts from exactly those initial/update assumptions, so this dependency is checked independently of the objective wrapper.

The objective-level structure requires `HasFDerivAt` only at the accepted iterates. It supplies the actual dot-product derivative, actual iterate displacement, Armijo, weak Wolfe, and exact full update. It does not assume a globally differentiable objective, gradient continuity, bounded Hessians, or iterate/matrix convergence. Its nonzero-gradient field matches the source's infinite admissible-run convention, under which a zero gradient terminates the run. The initial-only positivity induction is valid for every finite dimension.

The raw review theorems need only `c₂<1`; they instantiate the source margin as `c=1−c₂`. Omitting the unused lower Wolfe/Armijo constraints in these algebraic statements makes them stronger. Actual objective applications retain the source's full `0<c₁<c₂<1` convention. The one-step matrix theorem's positive `α` is likewise inherited from the report's positive-length run convention, not an extra algorithmic assumption.

For M.3, summability is the stated hypothesis. The lower-bounded-objective, finite-objective-limit, and compact-sublevel exports are additional objective corollaries deriving summability from Armijo. They do not replace the summability-only theorem. The bounded-gradient hypothesis is an actual `sqNorm g_k ≤ G²` bound; the source likewise permits choosing `G>0`.

The matrices `H` and `H₊` are not assumed to commute or have a common eigenbasis. The summation uses an eigenbasis of `H` and the general principal-sum conjugation theorem for `H₊`. No eigendirection convergence occurs in the argument.

## Indexing, nonvacuity, and interpretation

The types allow `n=0`, but a nonstationary run then cannot satisfy positive energy. `dimension_pos` derives `n>0` from the run, rather than silently adding it. Thus the sharp potential is positive through its trace term and its root can tend to one. Lean's totalized arithmetic at horizons zero or one has no effect: the finite-horizon claim requires `m≥2`, and the asymptotic proofs work eventually at large natural indices.

The source's `e_{n+1}=0` is an actual property of the principal-determinant sum: there are no `(n+1)`-subsets of an `n`-element coordinate set. The proof does not drop the top level or change dimension with the iteration. The review's literal finite-horizon wrapper also rules out replacing `m−1` by `m` in the source denominator.

This is a conditional theorem about each admissible infinite run, exactly as the source states. It does not establish existence of a weak-Wolfe point on every possible ray or prevent finite termination. It does not prove value optimality, `H_k→0`, `q_k→0`, or eigenvector convergence; M157 expressly disclaims those implications. The coordinate space uses the finite-dimensional Pi topology for derivatives and the explicit Euclidean sum of squares for the matrix estimates; this does not add a differentiability hypothesis.

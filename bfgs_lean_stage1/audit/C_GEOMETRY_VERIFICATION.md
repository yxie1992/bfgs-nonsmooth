Exact source coverage:

- `PlanarNormalGeometry` proves that a nonzero support normal places a point on the actual sublevel frontier; a shared normal supports the whole joining segment. The literal no-nontrivial-boundary-segment hypothesis therefore makes the supporting point unique. Nonnegative combinations of actual supports are treated directly, with a common positive pairing proving their nonzeroness.
- `PlanarNormalSectors` constructs the full Euclidean unit-normal sector from normalized convex combinations of two actual supports. Its compactness, unit norm, positive common-velocity pairing, actual sublevel support property, and disjointness at distinct points of one strict contour are proved. Norms used for normalization are explicitly Euclidean.
- `PlanarSectorContinuity` proves uniform positive pairing on a sector and uniform motion of the entire sector, with the interval parameter ranging over the full closed interval [0,1]. A fixed open neighborhood continues to contain all normals for nearby objective levels; this is not a pointwise-only parameter statement.
- `PlanarSectorCutoffs` constructs actual smooth cutoffs using separation of closed sets and smooth Urysohn. Closed supports are pairwise disjoint and remain inside specified positive-pairing regions. The final moving-sector theorem chooses the cutoffs before a common neighborhood of the level; every entire moving sector lies in a cutoff plateau, and velocity pairings are uniformly positive on its unit-normal support. Empty finite families are permitted. The displayed positive constants are indexed by the finite family and can be replaced by a single positive finite minimum when assembling the full field.
- `PlanarCornerCharts` applies the actual inverse function theorem to the map of the two branch values. The derivative is the actual gradient-row matrix and its invertibility comes from its actual 2×2 determinant. This constructs C2 level curves, local inverse parameterizations, local level-corner uniqueness, and C1 actual velocities. Common derivative pairings are obtained by differentiating the actual defining equations.
- `PlanarCornerFiniteness` proves that a local maximum of two differentiable branches is differentiable wherever their values differ. It then proves isolation of each regular nonsmooth point on its level and finiteness of actual nonsmooth points in a compact regular level subset. The compact set and its cluster set need not be finite, connected, or convergent.
- `PlanarCornerData` supplies the actual objective bridge: the constructed point curve has objective value exactly t, the actual coordinate derivatives of its branches are actual global supports of F, their pairings with the actual curve derivative are exactly one, and all sufficiently nearby nonsmooth points lie on the constructed curve at their own objective value. Neither a gradient alphabet nor a regular selected subgradient is assumed.
- `PlanarJoinedFrame` proves positivity and lower bounds for the literal finite height formula, using disjoint supports and values in [0,1]. It uses the actual angular derivative, proves smoothness in angle and 2π-periodicity of both height and frame, proves exact recovery of each level velocity on a plateau, and derives gradient visibility from positive height and positive gradient length.

The auxiliary `TwoBranchRegularAt` condition does not require branch convexity because the inverse-chart, finite-corner, and support-at-an-active-minorant steps hold without it when the full objective is convex. This is a stronger intermediate result under weaker hypotheses, not a weakening of the final source theorem or a proof of its secant bounds. Convex C2 branches remain part of the source assumptions for the later endpoint estimates. C2 input yields C1 level velocities; no C-infinity regularity of those velocities is claimed. Only the angular cutoffs and angular dependence are smooth to all orders.
